Section 2 — Fourier coefficients and bandlimit

The mean value of ff over the circle is invariant to rotations, and is easily estimated by averaging across samples. Thus, let us assume for simplicity and without loss of generality that ff has known mean 0. Passing to the Fourier domain, we assume that ff is bandlimited to KK Fourier frequencies, that is, ff admits the Fourier sequence representation f(t)=∑k=1Kθk,1fk,1(t)+θk,2fk,2(t),fk,1(t)=1πcos⁡kt,fk,2(t)=1πsin⁡kt, f(t)=\sum_{k=1}^K\theta_{k,1}f_{k,1}(t)+\theta_{k,2}f_{k,2}(t),\quad f_{k,1}(t)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\cos kt,\ f_{k,2}(t)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\sin kt, where {fk,1,fk,2:k=1,…,K}\{f_{k,1},f_{k,2}:k=1,\ldots,K\} are orthonormal Fourier basis functions over [−π,π)[-\pi,\pi), and θ=(θ1,1,θ1,2,…,θK,1,θK,2)∈ℝ2K \theta=(\theta_{1,1},\theta_{1,2},\ldots,\theta_{K,1},\theta_{K,2})\in\mathbb R^{2K} are the Fourier coefficients of ff. We assume implicitly throughout the paper that K≥2K\geq2, and we are interested in applications with potentially large values of this bandlimit KK.
