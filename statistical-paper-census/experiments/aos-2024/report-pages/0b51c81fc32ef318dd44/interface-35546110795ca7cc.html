Statistical Complexity and Optimal Algorithms for Non-linear Ridge Bandits

Section 1 — Sequential observation experiment

In this paper, we study a sequential estimation problem as follows. At each time t=1,2,⋯,Tt=1,2,\cdots,T, the learner chooses an action ata_t in a generic action set 𝒜\mathcal A, based on the observed history ℋt−1={(as,rs)}s≤t−1\mathcal H_{t-1}=\{(a_s,r_s)\}_{s\le t-1}. Upon choosing ata_t, the learner obtains a noisy observation of fθ*(at)f_{\theta^*}(a_t), denoted as rt=fθ⋆(at)+ztr_t=f_{\theta^\star}(a_t)+z_t, where {fθ:𝒜→ℝ}θ∈Θ\{f_\theta:\mathcal A\to\mathbb R\}_{\theta\in\Theta} is a given function class, and the noise ztz_t follows a standard normal distribution. Here θ⋆∈Θ⊆ℝd\theta^\star\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R^d is an unknown parameter fixed across time, and the learner’s target is to estimate the parameter θ⋆\theta^\star in the high dimensional regime where dd could be comparable to TT. Here the learner needs to both design the sequential experiment (i.e. actions a1,⋯,aTa_1,\cdots,a_T) adapted to the history {ℋt−1}t=1T\{\mathcal H_{t-1}\}_{t=1}^T, and output a final estimator θ̂T=θ̂T(ℋT)\widehat\theta_T=\widehat\theta_T(\mathcal H_T) which is close to θ⋆\theta^\star.
