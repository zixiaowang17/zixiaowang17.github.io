Theorem 3.2 (Online closure principle)

8 required APIs

Let 𝛟=(ϕI)I⊆ℕ\boldsymbol\phi=(\phi_I)_{I\subseteq\mathbb N} be an arbitrary family of α\alpha-level intersection tests. Then, the closed procedure 𝐝ϕ=(diϕ)i∈ℕ\boldsymbol d^\phi=(d_i^\phi)_{i\in\mathbb N} based on 𝛟\boldsymbol\phi defined by diϕ=min⁡{ϕI:I⊆ℕ with i∈I} d_i^\phi=\min\{\phi_I:I\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ with }i\in I\} controls the FWER at level α\alpha in the strong sense. In addition, if each ϕI\phi_I is an online intersection test and the family of online intersection tests 𝛟\boldsymbol\phi is predictable, then 𝐝ϕ\boldsymbol d^\phi is an online procedure. We refer to such procedures as online closed procedures.

Theorem 3.5

8 required APIs

Let 𝐝=(di)i∈ℕ\boldsymbol d=(d_i)_{i\in\mathbb N} be an online procedure with strong FWER control. Then 𝛟=(ϕI)I⊆ℕ\boldsymbol\phi=(\phi_I)_{I\subseteq\mathbb N}, where ϕI=max⁡{di:i∈I}\phi_I=\max\{d_i:i\in I\}, is a predictable family of online α\alpha-level intersection tests and 𝐝ϕ=𝐝\boldsymbol d^\phi=\boldsymbol d. Thus, for any online procedure 𝐝\boldsymbol d with FWER control there exists an online closed procedure 𝐝ϕ\boldsymbol d^\phi that leads to the same decisions.
