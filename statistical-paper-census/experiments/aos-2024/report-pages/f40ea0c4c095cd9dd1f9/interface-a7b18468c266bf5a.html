Section 4.2 — coefficient-shift model

In this section, we consider another type of data heterogeneity by allowing the regression coefficient 𝛃*\boldsymbol\beta^* to be different on different machines, referred to as coefficient shift. This setting is also known as conditional shift in some literature, since the distribution of the response YY conditional on the covariates (X,𝐙)(X,\boldsymbol Z) depends on 𝛃*\boldsymbol\beta^*. Formally, we assume that yi=sign⁡(xi+𝐳i⊤𝛃ℓ*+ϵi)y_i=\operatorname{sign}(x_i+\boldsymbol z_i^\top\boldsymbol\beta^*_\ell+\epsilon_i) for i∈ℋℓi\in\mathcal H_\ell, ℓ=1,2,…,L\ell=1,2,\ldots,L. Without loss of generality, our goal is to estimate the parameter 𝛃1*\boldsymbol\beta^*_1 on the first machine, and we assume that there exists a nonempty set 𝒜*:={ℓ:𝛃1*=𝛃ℓ*,ℓ=1,…,L}\mathcal A^*:=\{\ell:\boldsymbol\beta^*_1=\boldsymbol\beta^*_\ell,\ell=1,\ldots,L\} with cardinality (1−ε)L(1-\varepsilon)L, where 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<1. In other words, there are (1−ε)L(1-\varepsilon)L machines on which the coefficients 𝛃ℓ*\boldsymbol\beta^*_\ell are the same as that on the first machine, while the coefficients on the remaining εL\varepsilon L machines are shifted away.

To clearly demonstrate the strategy we use to deal with the coefficient shift setting, we assume there is no covariate shift in this section and the local sample size mm is identical for all machines, the violation of which is analyzed in the previous Section 4.1.
