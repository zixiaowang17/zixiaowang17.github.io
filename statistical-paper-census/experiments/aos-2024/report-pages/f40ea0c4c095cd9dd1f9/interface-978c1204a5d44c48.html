In RHC sampling design (see Chaudhuri et al. (2006) and Rao et al. (1962)), 𝒫\mathcal P is first divided randomly into nn disjoint groups of sizes N1⋯,NnN_1\cdots,N_n, respectively, by taking a sample of N1N_1 units from NN units with SRSWOR, a sample of N2N_2 units from N−N1N-N_1 units with SRSWOR and so on. Then, one unit is selected in the sample from each of these groups independently with probability proportional to the size variable xx.

Let us also assume that {Nr}r=1n\{N_r\}_{r=1}^n are as follows (see Rao et al. (1962)). Nr={N/n,for r=1,⋯,n, when N/n is an integer,⌊N/n⌋,for r=1,⋯,k, and⌊N/n⌋+1,for r=k+1,⋯,n, when N/n is not an integer, N_r=\begin{cases}N/n,&\text{for }r=1,\cdots,n,\text{ when }N/n\text{ is an integer},\\ \lfloor N/n\rfloor,&\text{for }r=1,\cdots,k,\text{ and}\\ \lfloor N/n\rfloor+1,&\text{for }r=k+1,\cdots,n,\text{ when }N/n\text{ is not an integer},\end{cases} (6) where kk is such that ∑r=1nNr=N\sum_{r=1}^nN_r=N. Here, ⌊N/n⌋\lfloor N/n\rfloor is the integer part of N/nN/n.
